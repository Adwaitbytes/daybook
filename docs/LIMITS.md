# Limits

What Daybook does not protect against, or protects against only partly, and why. Items marked with a review id come from docs/SECURITY_REVIEW.md.

## Accepted on purpose

- **Spam with real tokens (H-1 residual).** Accepted-token watching (the default) only reacts to logs from the accepted token contracts. Someone willing to send real 1-unit USDC transfers to a public pay address still triggers one billed reaction each (about 30k gas plus 0.000001 USDC on their side against about 0.04 REACT on ours). Nothing in `react()` can refuse a matching log. The defence is operational: watch `runway()` and the reaction rate on the health page and top up or pause invoice creation if it spikes.
- **Watch-all invoices pay for up to 16 junk logs (H-1).** That is the price of flagging wrong-token payments; after the sixteenth the invoice narrows to accepted tokens and further wrong-token payments are only visible off-chain.
- **Any reactive contract can make a till pay for failed callbacks (L-7).** The callback proxy bills the recipient before authorization runs. Mitigation is operational: keep each till funded for a few days, not a large float, and alert on `CallbackFailure` events whose injected sender is not our reactive contract.
- **`Paid` is not "money received" (L-8).** `Paid` is set from the origin log alone; `Settled` means the till swept and split the funds and acknowledged it. The pay page shows `Paid` as "Payment seen" and stamps Paid only on `Settled`. Daybook assumes Reactive delivers origin logs after the origin chain's finality; a reorg after delivery would leave a `Paid` invoice whose settlement ends Stuck.
- **Bounded manual-sweep walk (L-5).** When the owner sweeps by hand, the reaction resolves matching pending settlements only while it has more than 100,000 gas left. Anything it cannot reach is resent, fails against the empty pay address, ends Stuck after five sends and is closed with `resolveSettlement`.
- **Over-cap overpayment (L-6 residual).** `maxInvoiceAmount` stops over-cap invoices being issued, but an overpayment can still push one settlement above the till's per-settlement cap; that settlement fails five times, ends Stuck, and the owner sweeps by hand.
- **Receipt names one payer (M-2 choice).** A settlement's receipt goes to the sender of the largest single transfer on that chain and token, for the whole settled amount. With several payers on one pair, the others get no attestation; the ledger still lists every payer and amount.
- **Owner trust.** The owner can record a missed transfer (`recordMissedTransfer`, flagged by `OwnerRecorded` for verification against the origin chain), resolve settlements by hand, sweep any pay address to any address, cancel an invoice and keep what was paid, and authorize tills on the factory. The owner is the payee, so this adds no trust a payer does not already extend, but it is one key on every chain until ownership moves to per-chain multisigs (moving ownership does not change any address).
- **Pausing a till costs callbacks.** The reactive side has no pause; while a till is paused each open settlement is sent up to five times and billed on the destination chain before it is marked Stuck.
- **Determinism.** Pay addresses depend on the exact factory init code (including the metadata hash). Future chains must be deployed from the committed artifact, never a rebuild, and only on chains with the canonical CREATE2 deployer and standard address derivation. Changing the factory source (as the L-3 fix did) gives a new factory address, so a deployment of a later commit has different pay addresses from an earlier one.
- **Token quirks.** Fee-on-transfer tokens make settlement fail (the till receives less than it splits); only plain stablecoins are supported. Token decimals are supplied by the owner at invoice creation; a wrong value misprices the invoice.
- **Reserve events are spoofable.** `SavingsVault.deposit` is permissionless, so verification counts only deposits whose `from` is a known till.
- **`runway()` reserves.** Read with a low-level `reserves(address)` call on the system contract as documented in the economy page; if the call fails the view reports zero rather than reverting.
- **Fork test depends on a public RPC.** `test/security/EasReceiptGas.t.sol` forks Base mainnet through `https://mainnet.base.org` by default; it failed once with `InvalidSchema` from an inconsistent load-balanced response and passed on rerun. Set `BASE_RPC_URL` to a keyed endpoint for CI.
